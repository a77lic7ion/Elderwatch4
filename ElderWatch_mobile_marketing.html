A daily check-in your residents can actually use.

One tap tells you a resident is okay. No tap, by a set time, tells you they need a visit — automatically.

The morning round takes an hour. It should take two minutes.

Right now, someone walks the halls checking a card in every window. It works, but it's slow, and it depends entirely on one person's morning.

The way it happens today

Staff walk every corridor, check every window, and write it all down by hand — every single morning, rain or shine.

The way it could happen

Residents tap one button on their own phone. Staff open one screen and see everyone's status at a glance.

How the morning actually runs

Four simple moments, timed automatically, every single day.

1

Seven in the morning

Every resident's status resets, and a gentle reminder appears on their phone.

2

A resident checks in

One tap. Green if they're okay, red if they need help. That's the entire interaction.

3

Quarter past nine

Anyone who hasn't responded is automatically flagged — nobody is missed just because a phone stayed on the nightstand.

4

Staff take one glance

A single dashboard shows who's okay, who needs help, and who hasn't responded — sorted so the important cases sit right at the top.

Built around two very different mornings

One resident just wants to tap a button and get on with their day. One staff member needs the full picture in seconds.
